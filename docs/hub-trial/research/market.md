## 1. How wholesale brands run trial, sample and "risk-free first order" programs

**What the market has taught retailers to expect (Faire sets the baseline):**
- Faire gives retailers **Net 60 terms**, **free shipping on the first order**, and **free returns on the opening order with any brand** (60-day window, first order only; return windows can shrink based on account history) [1][2]. Small buyers now treat "first order is risk-free" as normal.
- **Faire Direct**: when a brand brings its own retailer through its referral link, the brand pays **0% commission**, compared with the standard 15% (2026, third-party source). The retailer still gets Net 60, free returns on the first order, and, if new to Faire, a sign-up incentive plus free shipping from that brand for a year [3]. This is the cheapest way to offer "risk-free" terms without carrying the credit risk ourselves.
- Many brands list **low or no minimums** on Faire. Faire runs retailer promos such as "save up to $100 on your first order" [4].

**Sample and test-box programs:**
- **Sugro UK retail sample box**: **75% of retailers who got the box ordered** the products in it (2023) [5]. Caveat: the boxes went to existing, already-vetted customers of member wholesalers, so a cold-outreach box will convert lower.
- **Unitas "Brand Box"** (UK): free for retailers to join, but **staying in the scheme requires a feedback survey after every delivery** [6]. Typical boxes carry 8–12 products matched to the store (aggregator summary, **unverified**).
- **Paid samples credited against the first order** are common in apparel and promo products. One company credits half the sample cost; another credits £50 per £500 ordered [7].
- **The counterpoint**: Fashion-Incubator warns against shipping free samples to unvetted requesters ("No reputable retailer will request such a thing"). It recommends verifying the business, documenting the terms and following up hard [8]. The risk matters most for high-value apparel. Ours is lower, because magnets and stickers are cheap and can be sold as-is, but it argues for manual vetting.

**Consignment norms (craft, museum and souvenir shops):**
- The most common split is **60/40 (60% of retail to the maker)**; 50/50 also occurs [9][10].
- The downsides for the brand are well documented: unpaid stock sits on the shelf, sales are reported late, damaged goods come back, and a shop with no money in the stock has little reason to merchandise it [10][11]. The guidance overall prefers wholesale [11].

**Abuse and cost controls in use:** manual approval of each application [12]; card on file for the opening order, with terms only after the first order (Lantern Press) [13]; first-order-only benefits (Faire) [2]; required feedback (Unitas) [6]; geographic or territory limits [14].

**Implications for our page/offer**
- Frame the box as **free starter stock the shop keeps and sells**, not consignment: no inventory tracking and no collections. The shop keeps the revenue from the box, which makes it a genuine test of sell-through.
- Cost controls to build in: a South Florida ZIP allowlist (already in the brief); **one box per business and address**; a **monthly cap**; storefront required (check Google Maps or Street View, Instagram, and the free Sunbiz company lookup **(unverified as an official vetting step; it is the state's public registry)**); a check-in call or WhatsApp at about **day 21**, the Unitas idea of feedback as the price of entry.
- The page states the deal after the trial in plain words, e.g. "Reorders are wholesale, minimum $X, paid on delivery or by card; terms after your first paid order", following the Lantern Press pattern [13].
- Optional later: list on Faire and send repeat buyers through **Faire Direct** (0% commission). Faire then carries Net 60 and the free-returns risk.
- Do not promise returns on the trial (it is free). For the **first paid reorder**, consider a cheap guarantee such as "swap any design that hasn't sold in 60 days". It costs us reprinting, not refunds (Form & Craft runs a 180-day returns guarantee for comparison [15]).

## 2. What independent gift and souvenir buyers evaluate

- **Margin is the first filter.** Keystone (2x, a 50% margin) is the *floor* for tourist gift shops, which need **55–65% blended margin** to survive the off-season. **Magnets and keychains typically carry a 3–5x markup**, apparel 2–3x [16]. Because cheap imported magnets set the shelf price, a local art line has to justify a lower multiple through **sell-through and uniqueness**.
- **Minimums.** Small indie brands commonly ask **$150–$300 to open** and around **$100 to reorder** (e.g. $200 to open and $100 to reorder; $150 for stationery-type goods) [17]. Advice to new brands is to keep minimums at about $250 or less [18].
- **Sell-through and reorders** are the proof buyers trust most: "will enough of them buy it that the order pays for itself". Benchmarks: **60–80% sell-through is healthy, above 80% is strong, below 40% is weak**. Reorder rate is the compounding signal [19][20].
- **Reliability.** Buyers want a brand that will still exist next season, ships on time, keeps quality consistent and **does not undercut them online** [18]. Lead time is set by each brand; what buyers punish is a missed date [21].
- **Displays and POP.** Lantern Press gives a **free display with a minimum product order** [13]. Counter displays drive impulse buys of low-priced add-ons [22]. Sticker brands sell counter racks (for example a 4-pocket counter display at about $70) [23].
- **Exclusivity.** Some brands protect a ZIP code, city or radius, usually conditional on minimum stock or reorders [14].
- **Location customisation ("name drops")** is the norm in souvenirs: the same design printed with the shop's town name, with minimums around **$175 or 24 pieces** [24][25]. Buyers in Key West or Fort Lauderdale will ask for it.
- **Room for new lines.** About 30% of independent retailers give **10–15% of inventory to new designers**, and 50% give 5–10% (Lightspeed; the date of the data is unclear, so treat as **unverified for 2026**) [26].
- **Paperwork for paid orders.** A tax-free wholesale sale in Florida needs the buyer's **Annual Resale Certificate (DR-13)** or a DOR verification number on file. Sellers can check certificates online, by app or by phone [27][28]. *Whether a free trial box triggers use tax is **unverified**; ask an accountant.*

**Implications for our page/offer**
- Show **unit wholesale, MSRP and the shop's profit per unit** for every product type, plus "this box earns about $X at suggested retail". Aim for **at least 2.5x** from wholesale to MSRP on magnets and stickers if costs allow (keystone alone looks thin next to the 3–5x category norm).
- State the **reorder minimum** (suggest $100–$150) and the **delivery time** ("ships from Miami in N days", or hand delivery in Miami-Dade if true). Being local is an advantage worth putting first.
- Put a **small counter display or rack inside the trial box**, or promise one free with the first reorder above $X. Products without a display get buried.
- **Keep our own retail prices at MSRP** on the storefront pages, so shops never see us undercut them.
- Offer **soft exclusivity**, e.g. "we limit how many shops carry us per street or block", conditional on reordering. Do not promise ZIP-level exclusivity in v1.
- Collect the **FL resale certificate at the first paid order** (mention it on the reorder page), never for the trial. Keep the trial form free of tax IDs, as the brief already decides.
- Note for the owner: the designs are Miami-specific, so Key West and Fort Lauderdale shops may ask for **name-drop versions**. That is a possible v2 upsell, not v1.

## 3. B2B wholesale landing pages: what converts small buyers on mobile

- **Mobile is the default.** About **50% of B2B search queries happen on smartphones**, and buyers who use mobile heavily move from lead to purchase about 20% faster (Google/BCG, 2017, so dated but directionally solid) [29].
- **Form length.** NP Digital (404 pages): 3 fields convert at 11.5%, 5 fields 8.1%, 7 fields 7.6%, 8 fields 5.8%, 9 or more 4.2% [30]. HubSpot (40,000 pages): extra **single-line text fields cost little**, while **text areas and dropdowns cut conversion** [31]. Mobile form rules: one name field, one phone input with `type="tel"`, correct `autocomplete` tokens (`organization`, `street-address`, `postal-code`, `tel`, `email`), and no optional clutter [32][33].
- **Section order on "become a stockist" pages**: value proposition, then requirements and eligibility, then the form, then **what happens next and the approval timeline**, then FAQ and contact. Approval is manual, with a stated time frame such as "decisions within 5 business days" [12].
- **Social proof that works in wholesale**: a count of stores carrying the line (Lantern Press: "more than 5,000 retail doors" [34]); testimonials that give the **store name and city**; photos of the product **on real shelves** (Lantern Press shows customer-location tiles [34]); marketplace ratings and badges (e.g. Faire 4.9 or "Top Shop" [23]); case studies with numbers [35].
- **Trust signals**: a real person and phone or WhatsApp; the legal entity; a clear policy on minimums, payment, delivery and returns [36][12]; a response-time promise.
- **WhatsApp is the native channel** for much of the Miami trade: an estimated 56–57% of US Hispanics use it daily (**trade press estimate, unverified**) [37].

**Implications for our page/offer**
- **Mobile-first order**: (1) a hero with the headline "Miami-made souvenirs that sell", three proof numbers and a **"Request a free trial box"** CTA; (2) bestsellers with **wholesale, MSRP and margin**; (3) **How it works** in 3 steps (request, then we review within N days, then the box arrives with a reorder card); (4) **what's in the box**; (5) proof: shops carrying us, shelf photos, one quote with store name and city; (6) the form; (7) FAQ (who qualifies, what it costs, what happens after, minimums); (8) WhatsApp fallback. Add a **sticky bottom CTA** on mobile.
- **Trim the form**: one "Your name" field; merge "phone/WhatsApp" into one `tel` input; **drop "store type"** (a dropdown that adds no vetting value); keep Instagram or website as the single optional field, because it is the vetting shortcut. Consider **two steps**: store name, email, phone and ZIP first (qualifies early and gives a partial lead), then the street address.
- Answer the buyer's three questions **above the fold**: *does it sell* (proof number), *what does it cost me* ("Free. No payment, no contract"), *how do I start* (CTA).
- Copy: "Request" and "we review every request by hand, reply within 2 business days" turns manual vetting into a trust signal. Name Jesús and give the WhatsApp number.
- Spanish matters here: keep the EN/ES toggle visible near the form, and offer WhatsApp prefilled in the page's current language.
- No real numbers yet, so **do not fake proof**. Until the owner supplies figures, lead with *product* proof (designs, "made in Miami", a shelf photo) rather than numbers.

## 4. QR reorder cards in shipments

- **Scan-rate benchmarks** (vendor data): product packaging in hand **8–18%**; direct-mail inserts 5–12%; **personalised codes do better**. **Placement and CTA copy are the biggest levers** (a 30–80% lift in tests) [38][39]. The CTA has to say **what the scanner gets**, not just "scan me" [38][40].
- **Print specs**: about 2 × 2 in on packaging (at least 2.5 cm), dark on light, error correction level Q or H if a logo goes in the middle, a vector or high-resolution export, and **test on several phones in shop lighting before the print run** [38][40].
- **Dynamic codes** (a short redirect you control) let you **change the destination after printing** and record each scan. Per-shipment codes give per-customer attribution [41]. Our own page with `?box=` already *is* that redirect layer. The risk moves to **URL permanence**.
- **Precedent for scan-to-reorder**: B2B distributors print QR reorder labels for repeat items (e.g. First Supply "First inStock"; QRwave) [42].
- Hosting note: third-party tests report that GitHub Pages **301-redirects** the `github.io` URL once a custom domain is set (**unverified in GitHub's own docs**) [43]. Renaming the repo or org, or moving hosts, would break printed codes.

**Implications for our page/offer**
- Card copy should state the benefit: "**Running low? Scan to reorder in 30 seconds. Your shop's details are already filled in.**" Put the WhatsApp number in plain text under the code as a fallback.
- **Two placements**: the card on top inside the box (first thing seen), plus a **small QR sticker on the display or rack**, which is where the shop is standing when stock runs low and the box is long gone. (This is an inference from the "placement is the biggest lever" finding.)
- **One code per box** (`?box=MB-014`). Keep the code non-guessable or short-lived if it maps to a store name. Prefer looking up the store name from the code over putting the store name in the URL.
- **Make the printed URL permanent before printing**: use a short, stable path (e.g. `/r/?box=`), ideally on a **custom domain** that outlives a repo rename or a host change.
- The reorder page should greet the shop by name, show 4–6 bestsellers with reorder quantities, offer a **one-tap email** (subject plus body with the box code) and a **WhatsApp button prefilled with the box code**, and mention "have your FL resale certificate handy". Use `noindex` and track view and click per box (as the brief says).
- Expect about 10–20% of boxes to scan at all, so plan **an outbound WhatsApp follow-up at day 21** regardless of scans.

## 5. South Florida tourist-retail specifics

**Volume:**
- Greater Miami & Miami Beach had **28.3M visitors in 2025** [44]. Greater Fort Lauderdale had **20.9M visitors in 2025**, with 85% hotel occupancy in March 2026 [45]. Florida Keys visitors spent **$3.6B** [46].
- Cruise passengers: **PortMiami 8.56M** passengers in FY2025, a record [47]. **Port Everglades 4.77M** movements in FY2025 (+16%) [48]. Pre- and post-cruise visitors are prime souvenir buyers.

**Season:**
- Miami: **February is the most popular month**. January–March is high season, November (Thanksgiving) and December (snowbirds) are busy, **April starts shoulder season**, and **August is the quietest** [49].
- Key West: **peak runs December–April**, with January–March busiest; July–August is the low season (travel-site figures, **unverified against TDC data**) [50].
- The hurricane season runs **June 1–November 30**, with the **peak from mid-August to mid-October** [51].
- Retail planning guidance says buyers commit months ahead of a season [52]. That applies more to imported and seasonal goods. Locally made impulse items get replenished in season, which favours a fast local supplier.

**Events coming up (traffic spikes):** Miami Art Week, Nov 30–Dec 6, 2026 (Art Basel Miami Beach, Dec 4–6) [53]; Calle Ocho, Mar 14, 2027 [54]; F1 Miami GP, Apr 30–May 2, 2027 [55].

**Where the shops are** (general knowledge plus travel guides; no shop-level data): Miami Beach (Ocean Drive, Collins Ave, Lincoln Road), Little Havana (Calle Ocho), Wynwood, Bayside, Coconut Grove; Fort Lauderdale (Las Olas, the A1A beachfront); Key West (Duval Street has "hundreds of shops", and T-shirt and souvenir stores dominate) [56]; Key Largo and Islamorada; Hollywood Broadwalk; Palm Beach and Delray (**unverified as souvenir-dense**).

**Trade shows:**
- **Surf Expo** (Orlando): Jan 6–8, 2027, and again each September. Trade-only, with a **Coastal Gift & Souvenir** category, 700+ exhibitors and 4,500+ retail buyers (resort, hotel, airport and boutique stores) [57][58].
- **Atlanta Market** (gift and home): about Jan 12–17/18, 2027 (sources differ, **verify**) [59].

**Implications for our page/offer**
- **Timing is now.** To be on shelves for January–March, trial boxes should land **between late October and early December 2026**, with the first reorders in December and January. Avoid launching outreach from August to mid-October in later years (hurricanes, slow trade).
- **Phase 1 geography**: Miami Beach, Little Havana, Wynwood and Bayside, where the designs are most local, then Fort Lauderdale and Las Olas. **Key West second**, because it is a 3.5-hour drive and Miami-themed designs may sell less there (see name drops).
- Event hooks for copy and outreach: "Art Week", "Calle Ocho", "F1 weekend". Shops stock up before these.
- Surf Expo in January 2027 is the one show that targets our exact buyer. Walking the floor is worth it, even without a booth, to collect contacts for trial boxes **(cost not researched)**.
- Cruise-port traffic (Miami and Fort Lauderdale) supports targeting shops near the ports and along Bayside.
